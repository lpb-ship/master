# Streaming consent, retention, and deletion policy

Status: **proposed v0.1 — provider evidence and reviewer approval pending**. Updated: 2026-10-30.
Source: [SUR-18](https://linear.app/lb-sandbox/issue/SUR-18). Accountable coordinator: **Logan Barragan**, issue assignee; reviewer and implementation-owner appointments remain open.

## Authority, scope, and evidence

This is the viewing-history extension to [SUR-6 data handling](quality-gates-and-data-handling.md#data-obligations-and-retention-decisions), not a replacement foundation policy. Processor locations, jurisdiction, lawful basis, legal holds, rights deadlines, log/backup limits, and release authority remain governed by Q3–Q7 there. [SUR-5](account-profile-contracts.md) owns identity, session lifecycle, and D3–D5 visibility/deletion contracts. Streaming records use its server-derived `appUserId`; connecting a provider never grants app identity or sharing rights. Sign-out does not disconnect providers.

“Must” below describes proposed implementation behavior **after approval**. Numerical limits are engineering proposals, not legal conclusions. A stricter approved provider or legal obligation prevails; a conflict or technically unenforceable limit blocks that integration until reviewers resolve it. Approval of this proposal cannot substitute for provider permission or foundation approval.

Evidence reviewed on 2026-10-30: the [streaming project requirements](https://linear.app/lb-sandbox/project/streaming-connections-and-viewing-activity-sync-3d5b951b37ac), SUR-18, [SUR-16](https://linear.app/lb-sandbox/issue/SUR-16), [SUR-17](https://linear.app/lb-sandbox/issue/SUR-17), [SUR-26](https://linear.app/lb-sandbox/issue/SUR-26), and the two foundation documents. The repository at `9430566` contained only README and the foundation documents: no implemented UI, API, worker, storage, or sharing surface. This inventory is a planning baseline, not a production audit.

## Consent and user-visible consequences

Before redirecting to provider authorization, show a separate, unselected app opt-in for importing the approved provider/profile's viewing activity and, only where authorized, subscription information. State the purpose (private history consolidation), field categories, available backfill period and sync cadence/limitations from SUR-17, recipients/processors and policy link, retention, withdrawal/deletion controls, and that provider subscription cancellation is separate. Refusal leaves the rest of the app usable and starts no import. Provider authorization is a distinct step; consent to import is not consent to share, advertise, or train models.

Record only the necessary receipt: `appUserId`, provider/connection and approved profile scope, purpose and scope version, notice/policy version, grant time, withdrawal time, and authorization outcome. Avoid unnecessary IP/device evidence. Security restricts receipt access; S2/S4 must approve its retention below. Imports require both a current receipt and valid provider permission. Reconnect requires a fresh explicit grant and new connection generation. Expanded purposes/scopes require renewed opt-in before collection; failed/cancelled callbacks create no usable grant. Never infer agreement from navigation, a preselected control, or successful login.

Proposed default: **disconnect deletes that connection's imported data**. There is no “keep history” option in this baseline. It would need a separate provider/legal retention decision and explicit user choice before implementation. Existing manually entered data and imports from other providers remain only where independently sourced and permitted; they must not retain deleted source metadata.

| Action / condition | Proposed behavior | Required message/consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disconnect a provider | Immediately deny further imports and access to its imported data, invalidate the consent grant, start token revocation and deletion propagation | Before confirmation: “Disconnect stops syncing and deletes history imported from this connection, including its contribution to combined activity. Your provider subscription and provider-held history are unchanged.” Show approved erasure/backups deadlines and pending/completed state. |
| Withdraw app import consent or confirmed provider revocation | Same deletion policy as disconnect, even without a UI session; invalidate jobs and grants | Explain that authorization ended, sync stopped, and connection history is being deleted. A new opt-in is needed to reconnect. |
| Expired access token / transient failure | Refresh only within the current approved grant; pause when renewal is required. No collection during invalid authorization; do not equate a timeout with confirmed revocation | Show action required or temporary failure, not successful sync or deletion. Proposed reauthorization grace is 7 days from first confirmed inability to renew, never extended by retries; then apply disconnect deletion. Provider-mandated shorter retention wins. |
| Delete a selected imported event | Hide the event, delete its imported contributions/derived copies, and suppress reimport of the selected source events for the current grant | Explain the scope and that this does not edit provider history. Independently entered activity is separate. Reconnection can import it again only after a fresh grant and explicit notice. |
| Delete all imports from a connection | Same as disconnect, so active sync cannot immediately repopulate deleted history | State that syncing stops and reconnection may reimport provider-held activity. |
| Delete application account | SUR-5 recent-auth/identity-proof rules and SUR-6 deletion workflow apply; immediately deny all app access/imports and fan out deletion across every connection | Distinguish immediate loss of access, live erasure, backup expiry, and any approved legal exception. This does not cancel provider subscriptions or promise erasure of a provider's independent records. |

An acknowledgement is not proof of complete erasure. Expose pending, completed, or action-required status through the authorized lifecycle workflow; do not expose deleted history to render status. Provider revocation failures must be disclosed without exposing tokens and include provider account-settings instructions where verified. No silent fallback to retaining history.

## Retention and deletion propagation

S3/S4 must ratify these limits against provider findings and Q6 before storage design or real data. Deadlines run from the accepted request or confirmed revocation, not the next retry; expiry cleanup starts at the end of the grace period. “Immediate” means an atomic denial recorded before acknowledgement; physical cleanup is asynchronous. Shorter category limits below apply even while the overall deletion request remains pending.

| Data category | Normal retention / minimization | Disconnect, withdrawal, revocation, or account deletion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Access/refresh tokens and authorization codes | Encrypted server-side, least-privilege access; codes transient and removed after exchange/expiry. Never in client storage, logs, jobs, or analytics | Disable import access immediately; attempt remote revocation where supported. Only the revocation worker may access quarantined credentials for bounded retries; destroy local token material within 24 hours, including on remote failure. Retain only a safe failure receipt thereafter, escalate remote failure, and do not claim remote revocation succeeded. |
| Scheduled, queued, running, retried and dead-letter jobs; cursors | Job payloads contain opaque references, not tokens/raw history; cap queue/retry lifetime at 24 hours (a longer backfill uses fresh authorized jobs) | Stop scheduling, cancel/purge pending work and cursors within 24 hours. Workers check account state, grant generation and deletion/suppression state before provider calls and atomically before writes. Discard in-flight results; stale callbacks or retries cannot restore grants/data. |
| Raw provider responses / staged records | Prefer memory-only normalization. If resumability requires staging, encrypt and restrict access; hard TTL ≤ 24 hours from initial receipt, not extended by retries. Exclude raw staging from backups | Immediately deny access; purge staging and temporary files within 24 hours. No raw payload archives, diagnostic attachments, or dead-letter copies. An inability to exclude staging from backup requires an approved redesign before collection. |
| Normalized events, provider record IDs/provenance, match candidates, subscription and connection metadata | Only fields needed for the approved purpose while the grant is active; shorter provider limits prevail. No indefinite inactive archive; Q6 must settle account dormancy. Refresh subscription facts only when supported; never infer them from authorization | Immediately remove from reads; erase live copies within the SUR-6 ≤ 30-day ceiling, with processor requests initiated within that window and completion tracked. Remove source contributions from deduplicated events and recompute from surviving authorized sources; delete events with no surviving provenance. No titles/timestamps from the removed source survive in combined records. |
| Derived data, caches, search indexes, exports and downstream copies | No new sharing/export surface authorized here. Inventory any later cache/index/processor; treat linkable aggregates as personal data | Invalidate access immediately; purge or recompute within the same live-erasure deadline. Send idempotent scoped deletion requests and collect acknowledgements. No deletion completion while an affected processor remains unconfirmed. Previously downloaded user copies cannot be remotely erased; disclose that limitation if an export is later approved. |
| Consent receipts, suppression keys and deletion ledger | Minimal opaque identifiers, scope/version/times/status only. A per-event suppression key is personal data, purpose-limited to preventing reimport; retain for the active grant and its deletion window. No raw titles, tokens, or payloads | Retain only necessary denial/evidence records through the last affected job/backup/export lifetime plus one day, then purge, following Q6. Disable the grant immediately. Any longer consent/legal evidence retention requires an explicit Q6/S4 purpose, deadline and restricted access. |
| Logs, traces, metrics, support and audit artifacts | Inherit SUR-6 telemetry allowlist and ≤ 7-day diagnostics limit; add only approved provider name and safe outcome codes. Exclude history/title IDs, provider profile identifiers, tokens, response bodies and consent text. Verify vendor logs too | Purge identifiable records where supported or expire within the approved ≤ 7-day window; no new copies. Investigate accidental sensitive logging, remove exposed content and follow the foundation incident process. Aggregation does not justify retaining linkable activity. |
| Backups, replicas and recovery exports | Inherit SUR-6 rolling ≤ 7-day backup lifetime and independent deletion ledger. Token secrets must be excluded or rendered unusable upon restore; raw staging remains excluded | Deleted data is inaccessible live, then ages out ≤ 7 days after live erasure (proposed outer bound ≤ 37 days from request). Restore in isolation, revoke restored tokens, replay grants/deletion/suppression records, purge affected data and revalidate authorization before workers or traffic resume. No restored job may reimport deleted data. |

The 30-day live-erasure ceiling is not permission to retain raw payloads or tokens for 30 days. Backup expiry follows the actual live-erasure time, not automatically day 30. Required processor deletion completion times and any unavoidable provider retention must be resolved under Q6/S4 before launch, not presented as guaranteed by this proposal. Lawful holds require a named qualified reviewer, exact basis/scope/deadline and periodic review; isolate held data from product use and inform the user where permitted. A hold never authorizes further sync.

Use a durable, idempotent deletion operation keyed by user, connection generation and scope. Record the denial and cleanup work durably before acknowledging; never accept a request if its enforcement state cannot be saved. Track token, jobs, raw, normalized, derived/processor, and backup-expiry outcomes separately with minimal receipts and retry/escalation ownership. A new connection generation cannot cancel an older deletion; old cleanup must not delete newly authorized data. Account deletion denies provisioning as required by SUR-5; later registration gets a new identity and no old consent or data.
