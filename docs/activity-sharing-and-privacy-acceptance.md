## Proposed consent and visibility model

**Granularity:** one future-sharing preference with an explicit set of included import scopes and, separately, manually recorded activity. No source is preselected. A new provider connection, account, or provider-profile scope is excluded until explicitly added. Prior activity is managed per stable occurrence, with a bulk selection of exact occurrences; service/date filters help select records but never become an open-ended historical grant. Per-friend lists, public sharing, and custom audience groups are outside this release.

**Audience:** mutually accepted, currently active friends only. Pending requests, followers, anonymous viewers, blocked users, and former friends are denied. Each future-sharing grant names the audience as “current and future accepted friends for activity occurring after they become friends.” An occurrence's audience is the accepted relationship generations present at its sharing eligibility time. A new or re-added friend receives no earlier activity unless the owner separately confirms historical sharing to the then-current friends. No subset targeting is offered. This prevents an unchanged old share from silently expanding to a new relationship; social and security reviewers must accept the relationship-generation contract under P2.

**Future versus historical:** a future grant starts at its authoritative server commit. Automatically eligible activity must both occur and first enter the app during an uninterrupted applicable grant, after that relationship began. A late import may qualify only if the verified occurrence time and first-seen time both fall inside that same still-active interval. Before-grant, paused-period, off-period, reconnect backfill, missing-time, coarse-time or boundary-ambiguous events require an explicit historical selection. Never substitute retrieval time for occurrence time. A correction/replay cannot promote an existing private event automatically. Resolve match, provenance, and household authority before sharing; otherwise keep the event private, including in a historical selection.

**Historical consent:** show the exact selected count, occurrences/date range, sources and current friend audience before confirmation. Freeze the selection and audience versions; if either changes, require a refreshed preview and confirmation. No “all history” checkbox implicitly includes later imports or new friends. Confirmation records an occurrence-scoped grant only for the previewed set. Historical sharing requires sharing to be on and unpaused; it cannot override a title hide, invalid source grant, or lifecycle restriction. A preview may explicitly offer to re-share individually revoked occurrences; confirmation atomically clears only those selected individual denials and creates fresh grants. Broader future-sharing changes never clear individual denials.

**Title hide:** a canonical movie hide covers all its occurrences across sources; a series hide covers all its episodes, including future episodes. The confirmation states this scope. Episode-only exceptions are deferred. Uncertain title/series matches remain private until resolved; they cannot evade a hide by using a raw title string. Explicitly making an occurrence private overrides broader future/history grants. Unhiding or removing an individual denial does not resurrect old grants: sharing an old occurrence requires a new historical confirmation. Turning off, pausing, or removing a source ends its active sharing interval and invalidates affected prior grants; resuming starts a new interval. Unhiding starts a new title-specific eligibility interval: both occurrence and first-seen times must be after unhide, so late imports from the hidden period remain private.

For a non-owner read, **all** must hold: authenticated active viewer and owner; valid source/lifecycle permission for every disclosed fact; current accepted relationship generation included in the occurrence grant; sharing on and unpaused; applicable explicit grant; no title/occurrence denial; verified ownership/match/provenance; and current policy versions. Missing, conflicting, unknown or unavailable authorization denies disclosure. Owner history remains governed by SUR-18 and is separate from friend visibility. Private means no attributable title, timestamp, count, existence signal, recommendation contribution or explanation leaks to another user.

Retain minimal consent evidence: actor, scope/occurrence and audience references, policy/notice version, action, server commit time and version, and withdrawal/supersession status. These are restricted personal data; receipt/denial-ledger retention comes from SUR-18 S2/S4 and SUR-6 Q6, not a new indefinite audit archive. No titles or full consent text in telemetry. No preselected opt-in, consent inferred from provider authorization, or automatic replay of consent after login.

## Behavior and acceptance matrix

Each row is a required test scenario after approval. “Immediate denial” means authoritative denial at commit, before acknowledging success; the separate propagation budget below applies to previously delivered app content and inaccessible materializations. “Future” always uses the occurrence/first-seen rules above. A privacy control changes visibility, not stored-history retention, except when the upstream lifecycle policy requires deletion.

| M02 Enable future sharing / add a source | Existing private activity stays private; existing grants in other unchanged scopes remain as authorized | Explicit confirmation covers selected scopes and audience; new interval begins at commit | New connections/profile scopes stay excluded; backfills need M03. A failed/cancelled enable creates no grant. |
| M03 Share selected prior occurrences | Only the exact previewed set becomes eligible for the then-current accepted audience | No effect on future occurrences or later backfill | Separate affirmative historical confirmation; invalid/hidden items cannot be shared. Reject a stale or partly invalid bulk selection atomically. |
| M04 Make selected prior activity private | Revoke selected occurrences for every friend, immediately | Other occurrences/future preference unchanged | One occurrence or explicit bulk set; persists across retry, reimport and ID aliases. A new historical confirmation is required to re-share. |
| M05 Hide a movie or series | Immediately revoke every covered occurrence, even if previously shared explicitly | All future covered occurrences stay private; hide overrides broader grants | Confirmation describes movie/all episodes and all sources. Alias/rematch handling cannot widen access. |
| M06 Unhide title | Existing occurrences remain private; old grants do not reactivate | Fresh affirmative confirmation allows only new qualifying occurrences under an active future grant | Offer historical review separately. While paused/off, unhide alone enables no sharing. |

| M12 Provider timeout/expiry, authorization dependency unavailable | Preserve stored state per SUR-18; deny affected reads when permission cannot be established | No sharing while required authority is unknown | A timeout does not fabricate withdrawal/deletion or a successful save. | M13 Remove/block friend | Immediately deny that relationship all past activity | No future access for removed/blocked relationship | Removal by either party increments relationship generation. Other friends retain only their authorized access; unblock/re-add never restores old grants. |
| M14 Accept new friend / re-add former friend | No earlier occurrence exposed | Qualifying activity after acceptance may be shared under an active explicit future grant | Historical access requires M03 to the current audience. Pending/follow-only relationships never qualify. |
| M15 Restrict/delete account, sign out, account switch | Restriction/deletion denies all sharing under SUR-5/18; sign-out alone does not withdraw saved sharing | Restricted/deleted identity cannot share; new account inherits no grants | Clear client identity-scoped caches on sign-out/switch; revoked viewer sessions denied. Do not confuse session exit with pause. |
| M16 Migrate legacy data / restore / rollback | Preserve only independently evidenced grants whose occurrence, scope and audience map exactly; otherwise private | Preserve a future preference only with equivalent, verifiable consent; otherwise explicit re-consent | Authorized viewer–occurrence pairs after migration must be a subset of those before. | M17 Failed, offline or ambiguous mutation | Last committed policy remains authoritative; never claim a failed revocation protected the history | No new grant from a failure or stale retry | Show failed/unconfirmed state, hide local sensitive content as appropriate, reconcile by operation ID/version. Read serialization and delivery fence below apply. |
| M19 Duplicate, correction, rematch, merge/split, delayed worker | A private source/alias never loses its denial through normalization | New identity resolution alone cannot create sharing consent | Reuse SUR-17 occurrence identity; carry denials across aliases. ## Failure, concurrency, and propagation contract

Mutations atomically compare expected policy, occurrence, source and relationship versions, commit the decision and durable propagation work, and return the persisted version/operation receipt. Bulk changes are atomic at the semantic boundary; a worker may materialize them later but cannot expose partly applied authorization. Use idempotency keys scoped to actor/action/payload; duplicate retries return the same outcome, while changed payloads conflict. Never use last network arrival to override a newer denial.

Authorization is rechecked at the final response/delivery boundary for every path, including cached results and background recommendations. If a reduction commits before that boundary, suppress/recompute the response. A response authorized and sent before the reduction is already delivered content, subject to the bounded app invalidation below. Reads beginning after commit must never retrieve the revoked content, even if physical cache cleanup is pending. Policy-store, relationship-store, version or invalidation failures fail closed; cache availability is not permission. A dead worker or dropped invalidation must not extend an authorization grant.

Inventory and test feed pages/cursors, profile activity projections, recommendation inputs/results/explanations, search/counts, direct event/detail and batch APIs, database policies, client/server/CDN caches, workers/retries, notifications, operational queries and restore paths. Bind cached projections to viewer and policy/relationship/source versions; private responses cannot become shared CDN objects. Pagination and materialized aggregates reauthorize on every page/read. Notification/email/push payloads carrying activity are deferred until separately approved delivery semantics exist; they cannot be treated as revocable app views. Operational access is least-privilege, purpose-scoped and audited under SUR-6, with no public service-role bypass.

V2/V3 load profile: production-equivalent staging and synthetic data; 1,000 accounts, 1,000 occurrences/account, 50 accepted friends/account, three included source scopes/account. Include skewed users with 10,000 occurrences and 200 friends. Use 20 concurrent clients at 10 total requests/second for 15 minutes after a 2-minute ramp; mix 40% feed, 25% profile, 25% recommendations and 10% visibility/relationship mutations. Record offered and achieved rates, queue time, errors, exact seed/distribution and provider/region/configuration revisions. Run three cohorts with cold, warm and deliberately stale caches; report each route/cache state separately with at least 1,000 timed reads per route/state. Run 30 N1 browser trials per route under that load, retaining SUR-6's runner and nearest-rank method. Unexpected read errors/timeouts must be ≤ 1%; expected denials are separate assertions, never counted as successful authorized payloads. Throughput shortfalls fail; do not discard slow/error attempts or pool cohorts to mask them. Q1 must approve higher capacity if the launch envelope exceeds this fixture.

V4/V5 protocol: at least 100 reductions for each of hide, individual/bulk revoke, pause, off/source removal, disconnect, friend removal and account restriction/deletion, at baseline and loaded conditions. Measure commit and final denial/removal acknowledgements using a common clock or documented clock error; sampling resolution ≤ 100 ms. Record maximum as well as p50/p95/p99, operation completion and missing acknowledgements. Exercise concurrent reads already in flight, cached pages, recommendation jobs, paused queues, crashes, lost/reordered messages, retries and policy/relationship outages. Every observation must satisfy V4; incomplete/missing evidence fails. Inaccessible materializations must be purged or rendered unusable for serving by 5 s; physical retention/deletion remains upstream policy. No extension of the deadline on retries.

Connected clients must clear affected visible content within 5 s even when an invalidation channel silently fails, for example by enforcing a bounded visibility lease. Offline/hidden/suspended clients must clear or conceal protected activity on loss of freshness and revalidate before displaying it on reconnect/focus; cached offline friend-history display is unsupported. Test suspended timers and reconnection, not only a healthy websocket. Screenshots, recipient memory, third-party copies, and bytes already delivered outside the controllable app cannot be recalled. Disclose those limits before sharing; they never excuse new unauthorized server reads.

Telemetry inherits SUR-6's allowlist, adding only approved aggregate deny/conflict counts, queue age, revocation duration and missed-deadline counters. Do not log titles, occurrence IDs, friend lists, source payloads or persistent user identifiers. Protected operation receipts may support scoped troubleshooting under the approved lifecycle; they are not general analytics. SUR-46 demonstrates role restrictions, alert delivery and safe incident diagnostics. A propagation incident disables affected sharing/consumer access until enforcement is verified; operations owns mitigation and product owns user communication under the approved incident process.

### Usability and accessibility protocol

Proposed minimum: **12 participants**, distinct from the implementation team; at least six primarily mobile and four primarily desktop users, four with low familiarity with sharing/privacy settings, two habitual screen-reader users (one VoiceOver/Safari and one NVDA/Chrome), and two habitual keyboard-only users distinct from those two. Other coverage may overlap; record the recruitment matrix and relevant experience without collecting unnecessary personal data. Use synthetic viewing history. SUR-45 must obtain P5 approval of the sample and rubric before recruitment; this small sample is a release usability gate, not a population-confidence claim.

Each participant performs and explains without coaching: initial opt-in versus historical selection; movie/series hide and unhide; pause/resume and paused-period activity; individual/bulk revocation; disconnect/reconnect; removal/re-addition/new friend; and failed/unconfirmed save. After each task, ask who can see which pre-action, during-action and future occurrences, whether storage/deletion changes, and what cannot be recalled. Score against M01–M19 with a frozen answer key; “unsure” counts as incorrect. Do not teach the answer before scoring.

Require at least **11/12 unassisted completions and 11/12 fully correct explanations for each scenario**, with at least 90% in each coverage cohort (round required passes up), and **zero critical misconceptions** that would cause unintended exposure, such as believing failed revocation succeeded or provider authorization shares history. Record participant × task outcomes and severity, not only an average. Any critical finding or threshold miss blocks the gate; fix and repeat affected scenarios with a fresh equivalent sample before acceptance. Previously coached participants cannot establish unassisted comprehension.

Reuse SUR-6's supported device/browser and WCAG 2.2 AA proposal. SUR-45 validates the privacy additions with keyboard, screen reader, reflow and responsive journeys, including focus restoration, labelled scope/audience controls, non-color states, confirmation, loading, conflict and failure announcements. Every applicable A/AA failure blocks under the approved target. Functional accessibility checks and participant comprehension must both pass; neither substitutes for the other.

## User-facing explanations required before confirmation

SUR-41/SUR-42 must express the approved matrix in plain language, including the included sources, friend audience, exact past/future effect and persisted state. Minimum meanings to validate under V6:

- Enabling shares qualifying new activity only; historical sharing is a separate previewed selection. Newly accepted friends cannot see earlier activity automatically.
- Hiding a movie or series removes all its existing shares and keeps future occurrences private. Unhide does not restore old shares.
- Pause removes prior shares and keeps paused-period activity private. Resume shares qualifying new activity only; past shares require a separate choice.
- Making an occurrence private changes friend visibility; it does not delete owner history. Disconnect follows the approved SUR-18 deletion policy, stops sharing that source, and does not cancel a provider subscription or delete provider-held history.
- Reconnect requires fresh import consent and separate sharing consent; backfill is private. New sources are excluded until selected.
- Saved reductions deny new reads immediately; already displayed app content clears within the approved 5-second limit. Recipients may already have copied content. Offline friend-history viewing is unsupported.
- Failed/unconfirmed changes are not saved protections. Show the last confirmed state, pending operation and safe reconciliation/retry route; do not render an optimistic success.

These are content requirements, not finalized UI copy or an implemented user interface.
