## Shared integration boundary

Reuse the foundation server session and streaming records. Do not trust a client-supplied viewer, expose the owner profile as a social profile, or interpret provider access, following, imported history or a known record ID as sharing permission. Cross-user reads remain denied until approved policy and dependencies exist.

Logical operation names and added revision fields below are proposals for review, not new transport endpoints or changes to SUR-17's schema. Privacy state should reference canonical records rather than fork their identity/provenance.

## Revocation and irretractable content

SUR-28 owns the behavior matrix: audience/granularity, historical opt-in, title-hide scope across repeats, pause/resume and paused-period records, prior-activity edits, friend removal, disconnect/reconnect and safe migration. These choices are **not yet agreed in inspected evidence**. Do not choose a default that expands sharing. Until accepted, cross-user exposure stays disabled. SUR-18's delete-on-disconnect is a pending lifecycle proposal, not an agreed social transition; a retained record, if later allowed, still needs separate sharing permission.

Proposed propagation protocol for G4 review:

1. The owning mutation service atomically records the new authority revision/denial and durable propagation work before acknowledging success. Failed persistence returns failure; ambiguous acknowledgement is reconciled by versioned readback. Visibility increases require fresh explicit authorization under the accepted matrix.
2. SUR-31 checks authoritative policy/relationship/lifecycle state on protected reads. Revision checks must cover authorization through output/commit; a race must retry or deny. A cache's TTL or an asynchronously delivered event is not evidence of current permission.
3. SUR-32 tracks affected owner, relationship pair, title/event/source/publication scopes and calls every registered consumer. Proposed event envelopes carry unique ID, schema version, monotonic scope revision, effective time and minimal opaque references. Consumers handle duplicates idempotently, reject older revisions, detect delivery gaps and reconcile against authority. Outages deny affected exposure; retries and dead letters never prolong permission.
4. Each C-row delivery owner evicts, deletes or recomputes its copies, checks current revision before refill/commit, and returns a durable acknowledgement or safe failure. Dependency indexes must locate downstream contributions. If provenance cannot identify affected outputs, invalidate the broader affected dataset rather than retain uncertain results. SUR-32 owns retry/escalation coordination; the store owner owns successful cleanup, including processors.
5. SUR-46 measures the maximum interval from committed reduction (`t0`) to every affected serving copy being denied (`tdeny`); track physical cleanup separately (`tcleanup`). Read-after-acknowledgement must not serve an old grant. Already in-flight responses and active client displays need explicit SUR-28 semantics and a numeric maximum delay; there is no invented grace period here. Record per-consumer timings, failures and end-to-end maximum under load, retries and outages. Missing budgets or unconfirmed consumers block release.

Sharing withdrawal is not necessarily deletion of the owner's private history. Delete source/activity/account data only under SUR-18/SUR-5; remove unauthorized social influence immediately at serving boundaries and perform physical cleanup within the accepted deadlines. The proposed SUR-18 live-erasure ceiling is not a cache exposure window. SUR-6/SUR-18 continue to govern tokens, jobs, logs, backups, receipts and holds; this document neither restarts nor extends their deadlines. A legal hold or old backup cannot authorize product exposure.

| Content destination | Responsible party / limit | Required disclosure and validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| App-controlled feed, profile, recommendation, notification inbox, cache or download | Corresponding C-row owner removes future access and stale derived influence; SUR-32 tracks acknowledgements | A saved change is distinct from cleanup completion. Verify old links, pages and projections deny after reduction under the approved timing rules. |
| Email/push already handed to a provider, lock-screen notification, recipient download, screenshot or copied text | Sender can request deletion where supported but cannot guarantee recipient/device/provider recall; sender owns accurate status and retention review | Explain before enabling sharing that recipients may retain copies; revocation limits future app access, not knowledge already acquired. A mutable link helps but does not retract a preview payload. Never claim remote erasure without evidence. |
| Offline or disconnected client with previously received bytes | Client owner clears app-controlled state when it can receive changes/revalidate; cannot remotely erase disconnected copies or user screenshots | Do not promise immediate erasure on every device. Durable offline support requires explicit policy approval; measure reachable-client clearing separately from disconnected-copy limitations. |
| Provider's independently held history or recipient's independent content | Outside app erasure authority; SUR-18 lifecycle messaging applies | Disconnect/revoke does not cancel a subscription or delete provider-held history. Independently authored content needs its own policy; do not retain a copied private source under that label. |

These limits do not excuse serving revoked data from app-controlled caches. Notifications, exports and offline sharing are conditional inventory entries, not newly authorized features.
